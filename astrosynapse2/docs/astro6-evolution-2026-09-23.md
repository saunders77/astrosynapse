# Astro6: diagnosing stagnation and testing direct policy search

The September 20 PPO supervisor trained for **3,300,864 additional games** in
about 65.65 hours without a new verified promotion. The September 23 replacement
searches the policy that actually plays greedily. It is running under
`data/progressive/evolution-20260923`, preserving inherited promotion error
spending. **It has now produced one independently verified promotion, to
Astro6 champion 10, and automatically continued searching against that champion.**

The candidate scored **50.389479% against champion 9 over 130,944 paired seeds
(261,888 games)**. Its time-uniform lower confidence bound was **50.001972%**.
Attempt 311 retained alpha `5.152939236540523e-7`; log evidence 14.6739045
exceeded the unchanged threshold 14.4785284. A separate scalar calculation from
raw outcome counts reproduced both the evidence and lower bound. All pair
indices were unique and contiguous, neither game was truncated in any pair,
and candidate/opponent hashes and the frozen runtime identity matched.
The audit is saved in `data/evolution-20260923/promotion-10-audit.json`.

This is a small verified improvement under historical rules v1, not proof of
continued future promotions or corrected-rules v2 strength. The winning candidate
was generated by the first direct-search version; revision 2 was installed
during its unchanged promotion test and affects subsequent search generations.

## What failed

The old campaign created 255 branches, ran 805 screens and 194 independent
confirmations, and reached only three new promotion tests. Its four recipes
averaged roughly 49.7–50.0% in screening. Confirmation means were also close to
50%. One broad-exploration branch was reused 39 times; an outcome-only branch
was reused 40 times. Increasing the amount of this training had not produced
reproducible improvement. The aggregate is saved in
`data/evolution-20260923/diagnosis.json`.

There is no demonstrated single numerical bug that explains all of this. The
observed behavior supports several narrower conclusions:

- The stochastic PPO training objective and the deployed greedy policy differ.
  Broad exploration produced particularly poor rollout results, while the
  conservative head updates barely changed the policy.
- Terminal rewards provide weak credit assignment. Short branches repeatedly
  returned to familiar starting points; replay anchoring also limited movement.
  These are plausible contributing mechanisms, not experimentally isolated
  causes.
- Selecting among many noisy screens creates optimistic scores. Independent
  confirmation repeatedly erased the apparent gains.
- The old supervisor stopped positive promotion tests below 50.5% at 32,768
  pairs, even when their declared budget allowed 131,072 pairs. That operational
  floor could discard small gains, but removing it alone did not solve learning.

Previous critic corrections and counterfactual training also failed to establish
a reliable gain. Small planning trials were expensive and supplied no compelling
strength result. A more complex algorithm is not justified merely by complexity.

## What the new search does

The search reuses the learned representation and directly perturbs action
rankings, ensemble head weights, and low-rank action features. It emits ordinary
NPZ actors plus equivalent safetensors models; Play and Arena need no special
policy wrapper. Critics and the state encoder stay fixed during these mutations.

Each population contains a parent, six positive/negative mutation pairs, and a
champion control when the learner differs from the champion. All candidates
play complete greedy games, with seats swapped for each seed. Common seeds
reduce noise in comparisons with the parent. Accepted search steps persist
across generations instead of restarting every short branch.

This is a testable simpler alternative to PPO, motivated by the empirical
results in [Mania et al., *Simple random search of static linear policies is
competitive for reinforcement learning*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.07055).
That paper does not establish that this game's policies will improve.

### First search results and revision 2

The first version accepted eight steps in its first sixteen generations. Their
selected matched gains summed to 3.76 percentage points, while the independently
tested learner remained close to the incumbent. Those selected estimates do not
measure cumulative improvement: they exhibited substantial selection optimism.
The GUI calls them **accepted search steps**, separately from verified promotions.
The eventual promotion established a much smaller 0.389-percentage-point score
advantage over champion 9; the summed selected estimates were not reliable.

Revision 2 adds an independent adoption stage and a combined proposal:

1. Screen the population on 128 paired seeds per candidate.
2. Combine up to three favored mutation directions, weighted by their paired
   screening differences. This is a convex combination of sampled actors.
3. Compare finalists, the parent, the champion control, and the combined proposal
   on 1,024 fresh paired seeds.
4. Freeze one nominee. Compare it and its parent on another 4,096 paired seeds.
   Adopt only if its matched gain exceeds two descriptive standard errors.
   A failed nominee cannot be replaced using that same adoption sample.
5. Independently confirm an adopted candidate against the champion on 4,096
   paired seeds before spending a promotion attempt.
6. Run the existing separately seeded, time-uniform promotion test.

Stages have different seed namespaces and durable records. Acceptance and its
next phase are saved together, preventing duplicate acceptance on resume. The
adoption criterion guides training; it is **not** a certificate of improvement.
There is no fixed 0.2% step floor or 0.75% confirmation floor in revision 2.

Revision 2 passed all 437 backend tests, the frontend build/render/type/lint
checks, and a two-generation integration run (896 search games; no promotion
attempt spent). It was installed under the campaign lock in runtime revision
`1790212872560140000`. Hash checks preserved all 120,448 completed pairs of
attempt 311, its candidate, seed and alpha, and the historical engine and
promotion statistics. Details are in
`data/evolution-20260923/installation-v2.json`.

## Statistical contract and limits

Promotion retains the bounded betting confidence sequence implemented in
`backend/astro2/sequential.py`, based on
[Waudby-Smith and Ramdas, *Estimating means of bounded random variables by
betting*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.09686). Pair scores lie in [0, 1]. For attempt
`a`, alpha is `0.05 / (a * (a + 1))`. Repeated looks are allowed; spending across
attempts sums to at most 5% for each incumbent's succession test. This is not a
5% guarantee for an unlimited chain of different incumbents.

Search, selection, adoption and confirmation results never count as promotion
evidence. Candidate hashes and evaluation identities are frozen. Pauses retain
the saved prefix. A failed or abandoned attempt remains spent. Positive tests
may use all 131,072 pairs; the existing negative-score futility rule remains.
No confidence threshold was relaxed to obtain a promotion.

The historical campaign still uses **rules v1**. Manual Play and Arena use
corrected **rules v2**. A promotion in this campaign establishes historical-v1
succession only. Portable artifacts do not make the two environments equivalent,
and their results must not be pooled. Verified winners also receive a separate
original-champion benchmark because successive wins need not be transitive.

## Operation and validation

The default resource budget is eight CPU workers, 96 cumulative active hours,
and 64 generations without promotion. Worker changes take effect between saved
evaluation blocks. The supervisor stops visibly on time, disk or stagnation
limits; the GUI can extend the time or generation budget. Rejected mutation
artifacts are pruned while recipes, paired outcomes, accepted learners and gate
artifacts remain available.

Pause/resume was exercised through the live API during attempt 310, with its
saved pair prefix unchanged. Before revision 2, all 432 backend tests and the
frontend build/render checks passed. The revision-2 focused tests cover mutation
portability, combined proposals, noise rejection, small precise steps, distinct
seed streams, saved acceptance transitions, counting and inherited evidence.
The in-app Browser connection was unavailable. Direct inspection in Safari
using Computer Use verified the live dashboard and exposed three display issues:
stale selection progress during promotion testing, zero active-generation games,
and near-one confidence rounded to 100.00%. The dashboard now shows the active
test and saved search-game counts, and formats near-one confidence as >99.99%. These changes were verified on the
live page. Saving the unchanged resource settings and resuming through the GUI
both succeeded. The resumed supervisor uses eight workers; a second hash check
confirmed that the saved promotion prefix and frozen inputs remained unchanged.

Runtime updates use `scripts/maintain_progressive_runtime.py` only while paused
and under the campaign lock. It verifies the old identity, saves a complete
runtime/manifest/state backup, and records changed hashes. Evolution revisions
can change the search module and supervisor scripts, not the frozen game engine
or promotion statistics. The full current source is not silently substituted
for the historical runtime.
